How this works

  • Thousands of possible futures. Each one plays out your plan year by year with random market returns: stocks and bonds have good and bad years, sometimes several in a row. The percentage is how many of those futures never run out of money before your plan-to age.
  • Everything is in today's dollars. Returns are after inflation, so $60,000 of spending at 70 buys what $60,000 buys today.
  • In retirement, other income (Social Security, pension, rental, part-time work) covers part of your spending, and the rest comes out of your savings, plus tax on what you withdraw.
  • What it leaves out. Changing tax brackets and account types, required minimum distributions, healthcare and long-term-care shocks, and big one-off costs. Real life can land outside every simulated future.

Many planners aim for roughly an 80–90% chance, with the flexibility to cut back in a bad stretch, instead of 100%, which usually means working longer than you need to.
