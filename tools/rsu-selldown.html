How this works

  • Three strategies, the same markets. Each simulated market is run three times: you hold everything, you follow your selldown plan, or you sell it all today. This shows how the choices compare in the same conditions.
  • Taxes. Each sale pays long-term capital gains tax on its share of the gain (federal + 3.8% NIIT if selected + state). At the end, whatever is still held is valued after the tax you'd owe to sell it, so every strategy is compared on an after-tax basis.
  • Markets. Each year, your stock and each destination get a random return based on the expected return and volatility above, and they tend to move together according to their correlation. When you enter a ticker, volatility and correlations come from up to 5 years of weekly prices; otherwise typical values are used. Expected return stays a conservative long-run assumption, because a stock's recent run says little about its next ten years.
  • What it leaves out. Short-term gains on shares held under a year, wash sales, dividends, fees, tax brackets that change with your income, and the risk your company's stock has that index funds don't, like a single bad year for the company. Real outcomes can be outside every simulated range.
